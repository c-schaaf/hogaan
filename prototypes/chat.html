My team isn't communicating and I think I'm part of the problem.
H

Naming yourself as part of the problem is already half the work — most leaders never get there.

Here's what I'm sensing: when communication breaks down, it's rarely about the words. It's about the room. Your team isn't talking because somewhere in their experience of you, they've decided talking isn't safe — or isn't useful. The fix isn't a better meeting agenda. The fix is rebuilding the conditions under which honesty pays off.

This week, try one move: in your next one-on-one, ask each person, "What's something you've been holding back from telling me?" Then listen without correcting or defending. Just take notes. If they say nothing, the silence is your answer — and that's data too.

What's the first name that comes to mind when you read that question?

Marcus. He used to push back on me and now he just agrees with everything. I think he's protecting himself.
H

That shift you're describing — from pushback to agreement — that's expensive for him. Disagreement was costing him something with you, so he stopped paying.

Here's the harder question: what did Marcus learn about the cost of disagreeing with you? Not what you intended — what he learned. Those are usually different.

When you sit with him next, don't open with "I want your honest feedback." That language has been spent. Try this instead: "I think the last time you pushed back on me, I didn't handle it well. Walk me through what that felt like from your seat." Specific incident, his perspective, you positioned as the one who got it wrong.

He won't believe you on the first try. The second time he tests you and you stay open — that's when the room changes.
